TL;DR

Vanilla trajectory DDPMs trained without contact supervision organize peak-contact topology into a sharp mid-noise lock-in window (t ∈ [600, 400] under a linear schedule). Inside that window, contact graphs become decodable from intermediate states and causally malleable under paired interventions — but target-directed controllability under naive classifier guidance does not follow: decodable gradients are not, in general, reliable control fields.

Abstract

Diffusion reverse time has become a useful microscope for structural variables learned by generative models, but little is known about what discrete physical structure is organized inside trajectory diffusion. We study peak-contact topology — the graph of which bodies touch — as a measurable order parameter for contact-rich trajectory DDPMs. Using vanilla DDPMs trained without contact supervision on three controlled contact-physics families (spanning two simulators, two dimensionalities, and distinct dynamical regimes, with 10–15 candidate contact edges and 449–559 realized peak-contact classes per scene), we introduce a three-axis protocol that separately measures readout, causal malleability, and target-directed controllability.

Across all scenes, contact graphs become sharply decodable in a common mid-noise band (t ∈ [600, 400] under our linear DDPM schedule); paired interventions show maximal causal leverage near the band's onset and strongest directed-vs-random specificity within it. Twelve controls — including a matched-SNR forward-noising null — rule out distance, label-noise, class-imbalance, probe-capacity, label-granularity, threshold-calibration, seed-specific, and pure-SNR explanations: reverse readout jumps exceed forward-noising jumps by 1.94×, 1.23×, and 3.76× on the three scenes. Yet readout is not control: across all scenes, training-free probe-gradient guidance is target-directed on the small subset of exact-hit trajectories but yields no statistically supported positive population-level target alignment under consistent-sample evaluation. The contribution is mechanistic — a three-axis operational separation of decodability, causal malleability, and reliable controllability, proposed as a template for studying physical diffusion dynamics — and methodological in showing that contact-aware planning requires more than naive contact-gradient steering.

Readout: Contact Topology Becomes Decodable in t ∈ [600, 400]

A time-step-conditioned probe trained on intermediate DDPM states shows a sharp jump in pair-F1 in the same mid-noise window on every scene: BD5 +0.089 (0.771→0.860), CD2 +0.162 (0.588→0.750), 2D-Disc +0.287 (0.360→0.647). Three independent probe seeds reproduce the transition to per-anchor pair-F1 std ≤ 0.013.

Causal Malleability Peaks at the Lock-In Window's Onset

Paired single-step interventions place absolute flip-rate maximum at the window's onset (BD5: t ≈ 650, M = 0.548; CD2: t ≈ 600, M = 0.628). The directed-vs-random specificity S(t) peaks within the window (t ≈ 500, ~5–6× on 3D scenes). Both directed and random perturbations lose absolute effectiveness as denoising proceeds, but random ones lose it faster.

Mode-flip rate vs. intervention step t. Sparse-grid (left, centre) and dense-grid (right) interventions on the 3D scenes; random perturbations (grey) are flat.

The Transition Is Not Pure SNR

A matched-SNR forward-noising null trains the same per-pair probe on q(xt | x0) of real trajectories — probe inputs at matched t have matched log-SNR by construction. In the lock-in window, the reverse generative pair-F1 jump exceeds the forward jump by 1.94× (BD5), 1.23× (CD2), and 3.76× (2D-Disc).

Reverse vs. forward-noised pair-F1. The lock-in window is highlighted; reverse generative dynamics resolves contact topology faster than forward Gaussian noising at matched noise level.

Takeaway. Reverse generative dynamics organizes contact topology faster than forward Gaussian noising at matched log-SNR — the lock-in transition is not an information-theoretic SNR phenomenon.

Readout Is Not Control: The Cross-Scene Controllability Gap

No classifier-guidance condition we tested has statistically supported net-positive target alignment on any scene under consistent-sample evaluation. Probe gradients are target-directed on the small exact-hit subset (ΔHam | hit ≈ +1.0 to +1.7) but the much larger non-hit subset is pushed off-manifold. At matched perturbation budget, narrow late-window guidance is statistically tied with full-schedule guidance on hit rate on every scene (paired-bootstrap CI for the gap: [−0.018, +0.018] BD5, [−0.020, +0.040] CD2, [−0.025, +0.010] 2D-Disc).

(a) Population-mean ΔHamming under the consistent valid mask, with bootstrap 95% CI error bars — no guided condition has statistically supported net-positive alignment on any scene. (b) Hit/miss decomposition: probe-grad Δ|hit (green) is strongly positive on every scene; the much larger non-hit population dominates the population mean.

Takeaway. Decodable classifier gradients are not, in general, reliable control fields — the timesteps best for paired-noise interventions and those best for classifier guidance need not coincide.

Cross-Scene Lock-In Window Alignment

Across simulator (MuJoCo / pymunk), dimensionality (3D / 2D), and dynamical regime (gravity drop / elastic billiard), the probe-derived readout window aligns on the same mid-noise band — the cross-family invariant we report as the central mechanistic finding.

Cross-family alignment of the lock-in window. The probe-derived readout transition coincides on t ∈ [600, 400] across all three scenes.

Contributions

  • C1. Contact topology as a discrete order parameter. The peak-contact graph is exactly observable from the physics engine, combinatorial even at small body counts (449 / 559 / 454 realized classes), and amenable to paired causal interventions.
  • C2. A three-axis mechanistic protocol measuring readout, causal malleability, and target-directed controllability separately, with bootstrap CIs.
  • C3. Contact-topology lock-in is cross-scene and not pure SNR. Twelve artifact-exclusion controls plus three DDPM training seeds per 3D scene rule out distance, label, imbalance, probe-capacity, label-granularity, threshold, seed, and pure-SNR explanations.
  • C4. Readout and causality do not imply control. No classifier-guidance condition we tested yields net-positive target alignment under consistent-sample evaluation; decodable gradients are not, in general, reliable control fields.
